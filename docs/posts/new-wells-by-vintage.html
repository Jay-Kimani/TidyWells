Are new wells getting better? First-six-month rates by year

November 2026Illustrative, not for reserves or investment decisions

Correction, 4 November 2026. An earlier version of this post sorted wells into "oil" and "gas" with a rule that labelled most Alberta and Saskatchewan oil wells that also produce gas as gas wells. That mixed the groups and its headline (Alberta gas wells starting 19% stronger) was wrong. The numbers and conclusions below use a corrected rule.

Drillers talk about better completions and longer laterals. If that is working, wells that start producing later should start stronger. We looked at the first six producing months of new wells in Alberta and Saskatchewan, grouped by the year they first produced.

What we did

  • New wells: spud date in 2021 or later, and not already producing in January 2022, so older wells returning from a shut-in are left out.
  • Measure: average daily rate over each well's first six producing months (oil in bbl/d, gas in Mcf/d). A well is "oil" if oil is at least half of its output on an energy basis (6 Mcf of gas counts as 1 bbl), otherwise "gas". We report medians.
  • Groups: by the calendar year of the first producing month. Groups with fewer than 30 wells are dropped.

1. Newer Alberta oil wells start stronger; gas is noisier; Saskatchewan oil edges up

Group (median)20222023202420252026 (first produced Jan to Mar)
Alberta oil (bbl/d)116 (n 1,762)134 (n 1,996)146 (n 2,080)159 (n 2,017)155 (n 621)
Alberta gas (Mcf/d)2,488 (n 944)2,356 (n 1,050)3,052 (n 896)2,686 (n 1,000)2,628 (n 298)
Saskatchewan oil (bbl/d)54 (n 1,133)54 (n 1,122)60 (n 994)61 (n 881)58 (n 318)

Alberta oil wells that first produced in 2025 started at a median of 159 bbl/d against 116 in 2022, a rise of 38%, and the median rose in every year in between. Alberta gas wells moved from 2,488 to 2,686 Mcf/d (+8%), but not steadily: the 2024 group was the highest at 3,052. Saskatchewan oil went from 54 to 61 bbl/d (+13%). Saskatchewan has too few new gas wells under this rule to report (fewer than 30 in every year but one).

2. Horizontal wells dominate new Alberta wells

Among new Alberta wells, 8,152 oil wells and 4,091 gas wells are flagged horizontal in the infrastructure file, against only 324 and 97 that are not. The horizontal wells started at a median of 146 bbl/d (oil) and 2,675 Mcf/d (gas), against 38 bbl/d and 71 Mcf/d for the others. The rise from year to year is probably linked to what is being drilled, but this data cannot separate lateral length, completion size or location.

What this means in practice

  • Do not forecast a 2025 Alberta oil well with a 2022 type curve; the median start is well above it.
  • Alberta gas starts are noisy from year to year, so one year's median is a weak basis for a type curve.
  • For Saskatchewan oil, older type curves are modestly low for newer wells.
  • Check horizontal versus vertical before comparing groups of wells.

Limits

  • Medians only; we did not adjust for lateral length, completion size, fluid mix or location.
  • The "oil" group in Alberta includes wells licensed for crude bitumen. The oil-or-gas rule is a simple energy cut-off; wells near it can be either, and condensate-rich gas wells are treated by their oil and gas volumes as reported.
  • First six months include any ramp-up and can be affected by early shut-ins.
  • 2026 holds only wells that first produced in January to March, and groups under 30 wells are not shown.
  • A well drilled in 2021 or later but producing before 2022 is excluded, which removes some of the earliest 2022 starts.
