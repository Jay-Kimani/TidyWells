What the public Canadian well files contain, how they link, and what each column means in the Tidywells loaders. You download the files yourself from Petrinex public data (Alberta, Saskatchewan) and the BC Energy Regulator data centre; we do not host them.

2. How they link

In Alberta and Saskatchewan, a production row points to a well by FromToID (the well ID), the infrastructure file ties that well ID to a licence number, and the licence file gives the licensee. Both licence numbers must be read as text and padded to seven characters to match reliably. In BC, wells are keyed by UWI and the index has no operator names.

4. Units and conventions

  • Liquids in cubic metres (m3), gas in thousands of cubic metres (103m3). Tidywells converts to barrels (×6.28981) and thousand cubic feet (×35.3147).
  • Alberta and Saskatchewan volumes are monthly totals. Daily rates divide by calendar days; BC rates can use producing days.
  • Alberta “oil” includes wells licensed for crude bitumen; filter on the licence substance if you want them apart.
  • The same company can appear under several names; join on the licensee code instead.

6. Short glossary

TermMeaning
Decline curveA formula that projects how a well's production rate falls with time.
Arps hyperbolicThe standard decline-curve family: rate = qi / (1 + b·Di·t)1/b.
qiStarting rate of the curve.
DiNominal initial decline rate, per year.
b-factorControls how fast the decline slows; b = 0 is exponential.
Terminal declineThe slow, steady decline rate a curve is switched to late in life.
NPVNet present value: future cash flows discounted to today.
GORGas-oil ratio: gas produced per barrel of oil, in scf/bbl.
Shut-inA well not producing but not abandoned.
LicenseeThe company named on a well licence.
UWIUnique well identifier used in BC.
Spud dateThe date drilling began.
